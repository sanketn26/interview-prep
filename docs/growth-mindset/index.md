# Growth Mindset

Not a rehearsed story. The thing you actually do when nobody's grading you for it.

---

## Why This Exists

[Behavioural](../behavioural/index.md) teaches you to narrate judgement you already have — six well-told stories, STAR plus reflection, scoped to the level you're interviewing for. This section is upstream of that: **it's about whether you actually have the judgement, not whether you can narrate it.** A candidate can memorize a perfect "I pushed back on my manager" story and still, in the room three months later, fold the first time a VP raises their voice. The story and the character aren't the same thing, and only one of them is fakeable for forty-five minutes.

None of this is corporate-poster material. It's the specific, unglamorous mechanics of staying an engineer worth working with over a fifteen-year career, not just a good interview performance once.

---

## Mental Model: Two Different Failure Modes

Almost every career-damaging mistake in this space falls into one of two buckets, and they require opposite corrections:

```
TOO SOFT                                    TOO RIGID
─────────                                    ─────────
Says yes to everything            Digs in on everything, including
Absorbs blame that isn't theirs   things that don't matter
Never names the real cost         Confuses "I disagree" with
Burns out quietly, resents        "this is a hill to die on"
  loudly, six months later        Every disagreement becomes
Mistakes agreeableness            a character test
  for professionalism             Mistakes rigidity for integrity

                    ↓                              ↓
         Both produce the same outcome:
         people stop trusting your judgement,
         for opposite reasons.
```

The skill this whole section is built around is **calibration** — knowing which situations are worth spending your credibility on, and which aren't, and being honest with yourself about which bucket you're defaulting to under pressure. Most people have a default direction (some fold, some dig in), and the correction is almost always toward the *opposite* of your default, not toward a fixed rule.

## Key Takeaways

- **Judgement and the ability to narrate judgement are different skills** — this section builds the first one; [Behavioural](../behavioural/index.md) teaches the second.
- **Most people default toward either too soft or too rigid under pressure** — the fix is almost always toward your opposite, not toward a universal rule.
- **None of this is about being liked.** It's about staying someone whose judgement other engineers actually trust, which is a much narrower and more durable target.
